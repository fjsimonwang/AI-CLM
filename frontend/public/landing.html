on one record.

What it does

Four things
nobody else does
this well.

Not a chat box bolted onto a repository. These are the places where the AI actually carries the work — and where you can still see exactly what it did.

01

Conversational intake

Describe what you need in plain language. The assistant asks only what is missing, fills every field with provenance and a confidence score, scores your precedents, predicts the routing, and assembles the draft from your templates and playbook. Drop in third-party paper and it reads that instead.

02

Agent Crew

Every participant on a contract can have an agent that speaks for them in the discussion — in the first person, grounded in the record. Ask a question and it routes itself to whoever is best placed to answer: finance, legal, the approver. Agents advise. People still decide.

03

Auto-rejection

Approvers describe a gate in their own words. The AI restates it as a numbered list of executable requirements — attachment, field and text checks — that you can dry-run against a live task before it is ever allowed to fire. Bad requests stop before they reach your queue.

04

Portfolio inquiry

Ask your portfolio a question in one line. You get the filtered register, the AI's restatement of what it understood, editable filters when it read you wrong — and a chart you can pin straight to your dashboard.

The stack

Seven layers.
One record.

A contract here is not a file with metadata stapled to it. It is a stack — and every layer knows the line of text it came from.

  1. The drafted contract itself — assembled from your template and clause playbook, edited in place, rebuilt from the template whenever you want it re-assembled.

  2. Every extracted term with the sentence it came from, whether it was inherited or overridden, and how confident the extraction was.

  3. Which playbook position each clause came from, and at which tier: preferred, acceptable, fallback or unacceptable.

  4. The approval path, who holds the current task, every outcome, and the note behind each approve or reject.

  5. What has to happen once it is live — dates, owners, status — each carrying the line of text it was extracted from.

  6. The risk register. Every AI review finding lands here with its severity and its quoted location in the document.

  7. Who did what and when, with AI actors marked — and everything the AI wrote reversible from the activity log.

Every team

One platform.
Six sets of problems.

Contracts are never one team's job. The platform holds the whole path end to end — and shows each person only the slice they are cleared for.

Stop being the bottleneck.

Playbook tiers, clause variants and AI document review turn every request into a decision you can make in minutes rather than an afternoon of reading.

  • Deviations are quoted, ranked by severity and written straight to the risk register.
  • Paste counterparty language and get its closest playbook tier with the reasoning.
  • An approver briefing on every task: what changed, what deviates, what is being asked of you.

Vendor paper, on your terms.

Third-party paper stops being a black box. Upload it and the platform reads the document, fills the record and checks it against your playbook before an approver ever opens it.

  • Supporting documents travel with the request — including through a revise-and-resubmit.
  • Counterparty master data carries type and sanctions status.
  • Auto-rejection rules can require the diligence pack before the request reaches you.

Value you can actually see.

Signing authority, thresholds and contract value roll up into a register you can filter, sort, chart and pin — with expiry and renewal on one pipeline.

  • Per-column filters and sorts on the register; your layout persists.
  • Signing authority modelled per entity, user, type and maximum value.
  • Save any inquiry as a live dashboard chart.

Ship the deal.

One request, plain language, a few minutes. No form to decode, no guessing which template applies, no waiting to find out who has to sign it.

  • Start from a recent contract: new one like this, same party new type, or amend it.
  • The expected routing and its score are shown before you submit.
  • Returned request? Revise and resubmit updates the same contract — same number, no duplicates.

Obligations don't slip.

Every obligation is extracted with its source quote and a confidence score, then owned, dated, verified and closed out — with overdue in red where nobody can miss it.

  • Low-confidence extractions are queued for human verification, not silently trusted.
  • Policies and procedures are tagged by role, country and region — the assistant answers from them.
  • Every AI interaction lands in a governance log you can filter and revert.

Family trees, detected.

Amendments, masters and statements of work rarely arrive labelled. The platform proposes the links and waits for a human to confirm them.

  • AI-detected relations arrive with a confidence score and its reasoning.
  • Confirm or dismiss — nothing is linked behind your back.
  • Migrated contracts are badged, with unverified low-confidence terms surfaced up front.

Our belief

People should keep the judgement. Everything else is negotiable.

Contract software spent twenty years digitising the filing cabinet. It got very good at storage and never touched the work itself — the reading, the checking, the chasing, the decisions made at eleven at night because someone needed an answer before a call in the morning.

So we built the other half. Agents that sit inside the record rather than beside it in a chat window, doing the reading and the chasing, speaking for the people they represent, and stopping precisely where judgement begins.

And because an agent you cannot audit is an agent you cannot trust, every single thing the AI does here is logged, attributable and reversible. Prompt version, confidence, latency, outcome. One table. One click to undo.

That is the whole idea. Spend the day on the two percent that actually needs you.

Governance

Auditable
by design.

AI you cannot inspect has no place near a contract. Every interaction is recorded with the prompt version behind it, its confidence and its outcome — and anything the AI wrote can be taken back.

Full AI activity log

When, surface, capability, prompt id and version, confidence, latency, outcome. One table, every interaction, nothing hidden behind a summary.

One-click revert

Anything the AI wrote to a contract can be undone from the log — and the audit trail records who undid it, and when.

Access-scoped knowledge

Humans hold the pen

Agents contribute to discussions. They never approve, never sign and never change a contract. Approvals, signatures and closures stay with people.
